Checkerboard Hill

3rd March 2020 - Kowloon City, Hong Kong

The lockdown has been in full force since February, and we’ve been doing online school for the past weeks. They closed all the fields, so me and my friends meet every week at the park on checkerboard hill, a thirty minute walk from home.

At the park, if you walk along the drain outside of the park fence, you eventually reach a trail, then the small hill on top of the park, wrap around the back to go past the water treatment plant, then arrive at the checkerboard side. The entire mountain was painted in giant red and white squares which stood in the direction of Victoria Harbor. Sunsets were always the prettiest up here, especially with friends.

I learned recently that the giant checkerboard was here because this hill used to be an important waypoint for navigation into Kai Tak International Airport, which was where the airport used to be. I live next to it now, and it’s a giant construction site that stretches out to the harbor.

Every time I walk through Kowloon City and think about how low the buildings were to accommodate for the landing planes. I think about how my parents used to come to this area to travel internationally, and I think about how pilots flying the planes would see checkerboard hill, and know that they were about to arrive in Hong Kong.
