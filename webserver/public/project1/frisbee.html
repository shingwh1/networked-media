I'm so grateful for frisbee!

23rd August 2026 - Limerick, Ireland

Having started frisbee so young, it always felt like a constant to me. It wasn’t whether or not I was going to play when I got to the U.S. for college even if there wasn’t a school team at SVA, it was how and where I was going to play once I got to New York.

Like all things in life though, do enough of it competitively and you’ll begin to fall out of love. I heard somewhere that in order to love a sport, it has to take something away from you and you have to learn to navigate between choosing the sport and choosing yourself. Before going to Ireland for worlds, I think I reached a point of indifference, which was arguably worse. I didn’t care for the outcome of games, I didn’t even really care that much about getting better anymore. It felt like I had fallen out of love.

Then I got to Ireland, where it was a week of frisbee nonstop with friends from home, friends I made in the U.S., and friends I made at the tournament. I’m not religious, but there is something spiritual in attending such a large tournament, where hundreds of people who have dedicated years of their lives to this sport come together and share their passion for the same thing.

Coming out of the tournament, I feel alive again. Especially nowadays, I feel like it’s particularly hard to be optimistic given the state of the world, but somehow in a country across the Atlantic with hundreds of strangers who love chasing a piece of plastic flying through the air, I felt like I belonged to something bigger than myself.
